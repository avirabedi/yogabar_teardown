Slide 2 · what I did & what I can't

Method & the honest limitation

How I did it

Scraped every active ad on each brand's Meta page for India, sorted by Meta's own impression ranking. For each ad I captured the full copy, headline, CTA, landing URL, format, launch date and how many near-duplicate copies it runs, then tagged it to a fixed codebook so the library reports which argument works, not just which ad exists.

5 brandsIndia · active only

Two limitations, stated upfront

1. No performance data. India's Ad Library shows no spend, impressions, CTR or ROAS for commercial ads. So "is it working?" is inferred from how long an ad has survived, how many copies run, and refresh velocity (how fast the brand launches new ads). Brands kill losers fast, so a 3 to 4 month old ad is almost certainly profitable.

2. Hooks are read from captured text, not watched frame-by-frame. I classified each hook from the ad's lead line / on-screen text as the Library stores it. A true first-3-seconds audit means watching each video, every card links to the live ad so you can verify.

Before the ads · where angles come from

Research in a nutshell

Good angles aren't invented, they're found. The Creative Systems method says they come from four places: what customers say, what your own winners share, what competitors keep paying to run, and the category's conventions. Here's that scan for Yoga Bar.

Customer voice · reviews & support

The objections, in their words

The clearest proof of the category's real objections is what the longest-running competitor ads choose to answer, you don't pay for months to handle an objection nobody has:

"…if protein powder bloats you, that's not on you." → the bloat / digestion objection
"Protein for beginners. Finally." → the "this isn't for me / gym-bros only" objection
"…tastes like ACTUAL food." → the chalky / fake-flavour objection
"We publish our recipes. Every %." → the trust / what's-really-inside objection

Yoga Bar's own reviews & support tickets are the ideal next input, the exact-word source a brand team should mine on day one.

Your own winners

What Yoga Bar's survivors share

  • Its single longest-running ad (238d) is a plain static: "Pure 25g Protein. No Junk. No Bloat.", specific claim, one product, no mood.
  • The dual-protein science line (whey + fermented yeast) recurs across its steadiest video.
  • Pattern: Yoga Bar's winners are specific and functional, yet most of the account is generic catalog. It already knows what works; it just doesn't do more of it.
Competition · ad library

What rivals keep paying to run

  • The Whole Truth has run a transparency line for 275 days, it has decided "clean, proven, nothing to hide" is worth repeating.
  • SuperYou keeps a gut-friendly protein claim + discount alive at huge volume.
  • Max Protein is betting on a celebrity "No Cheating" platform (Kartik Aaryan).
  • The survivors tell you the arguments the category has validated, and which are already taken.
Category conventions · what to break

What everyone does the same

  • Open on a flat claim ("Xg protein, no added sugar"), 96% of all opening lines.
  • Lean on dynamic catalog and quick-commerce for volume.
  • Compete on price and flavour, not belief.
  • The convention is the opportunity: the brand that opens differently and argues a real reason-to-believe stands out by contrast.

Hooks, the opening that earns attention

Under Meta's ranking, the first frame decides how much distribution your money buys. A hook is what the ad says first, its opening line or on-screen text, not the caption. Here's the hook family every brand opens with.

Where Yoga Bar can bid next

The hook families almost nobody in the category uses, each an open lane:

Problem-agitate. Open on the pain ("Bloated by 4pm again?") before the product. Used by a handful of TWT/Phab ads; Yoga Bar: zero.
Demonstration. Show it working in frame one, the mix test, the pour, the texture. Almost unused category-wide.
Question. Direct address ("Kabhi socha why some proteins bloat you?"). One TWT ad does this; it's wide open.
Myth-bust / negative. "Stop buying protein by the price tag." Cuts through a wall of claims.

Yoga Bar opens 57 of 59 argument-ads on a flat claim. The cheapest win here is a hook test: same body, five different openings.

Positioning · competitor research + own analysis

What Yoga Bar leaves on the table

Crowded, everyone is here

Gut-friendly / no-bloat protein. Yoga Bar, SuperYou and TWT all crowd this. Yoga Bar's own long-runners live here.
Functional claim (Xg protein, no sugar). The category default, table stakes, not a differentiator.
Taste-vs-health. Phab owns "cheat/dessert"; Max Protein has the celebrity "No Cheating" platform. A loud corner.
Quick-commerce convenience. Where Yoga Bar already competes hard (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart). But it's a distribution message, not a reason to believe.

Open, nobody (or only one) owns it

Ingredient transparency & proof. The Whole Truth owns it alone. Yoga Bar & SuperYou run it zero times, despite Yoga Bar's ITC-grade QC.
Problem-agitate hook. Barely used by anyone, the strongest opening, unclaimed.
Founder & origin. Yoga Bar's sisters-to-ITC story is one of the best in Indian D2C and appears in zero ads.
Social proof & comparison. Only The Whole Truth uses reviews and category education. The strongest trust levers, largely open.
The bottom line: Yoga Bar owns real proof, ITC-grade quality control, a clean-label formula, an Indian-gut protein, a founder story and thousands of ratings, and puts none of it in the ad, on the PDP or on the quick-commerce tile, the exact places people decide to buy. It is spending on reach and discounts while The Whole Truth compounds a transparency story Yoga Bar could out-resource overnight.
Fix this week

Craft & formatting fixes

Positioning is the long game. These are format and craft problems in Yoga Bar's live account right now, cheap to fix, worth fixing before the brand work even lands.

01

89% of the account is catalog

Only 8 of 75 Yoga Bar ads are purpose-built creative; the rest are dynamic product ads with templated copy like {{product.name}}. Stand up a crafted-video pipeline.

02

Video is just 9%

7 of 75 ads are video, almost nothing built for Reels and Stories, where the reach is. Ship 9:16 vertical masters, not resized Feed squares.

03

Every ad opens on a claim

57 of 59 argument-ads open with a flat product claim. Add question, problem and demo hooks so the first frame earns the scroll.

04

The same line runs up to 6×

Near-identical copy is duplicated across ads. Meta groups near-duplicates and splits their learning, so copies fight each other. Consolidate.

05

18 price-led ads, no proof

Eighteen ads lead on "₹999" with nothing behind the number. That trains people to wait for a discount and chips at the premium. Pair every price with one proof point.

Where the creative clashes with Meta's UI

Illustrative (I can't pull each live creative's pixels here). In Reels & Stories, Meta paints its own interface over your ad: the top ~14% (profile name) and the bottom ~35% (caption, like, comment, share, CTA). Put the logo, claim, price or CTA in the red zones and most viewers never see it. This is the pattern to check every Yoga Bar creative against.

Safe-area & aspect-ratio check

In Reels & Stories, Meta's own UI sits on top of the creative: the top ~14% (profile name), the bottom ~20% (Stories) to 35% (Reels) (caption, like, comment, share, CTA) and about 6% each side. Anything important there is covered.

The risk in Yoga Bar's account: with 9% video and the rest 1:1 / catalog assets, most inventory isn't built 9:16, a square asset dropped into Reels puts the logo, claim or price straight under the caption and buttons.
9:16 for Reels & Stories, 4:5 for Feed. Never letterbox a square into Reels, it reads as lazy and gets throttled.
Keep logo, claim, price and CTA inside the middle ~66%. Put the hook as on-screen text high-centre in the first frame, most viewers watch sound-off.

A per-ad pixel audit needs the creative files; this is the checklist to run against them, and the reason to rebuild as 9:16.
